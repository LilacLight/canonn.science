At approximately 09:00 UGT on the 29th October 3312, the Pilots' Federation sent out a request for commanders to investigate the permit locked LFT 509 system.

LFT 509 INVESTIGATION

Commander,
The Pilots' Federation has been asked to investigate the LFT 509 system.

This system has been permit-locked for almost sixty years, for reasons that were classified at the time. An anonymous source, trusted by senior figures in the Pilots' Federation, has arranged for permit access to be given to licensed Commanders only. We want you to find out what happened there.

We don't know what you can expect to find, so make sure you are equipped for both ship and SRV exploration.

Oh-seven,
The Pilots' Federation

Investigating LFT 509

Upon arriving within the LFT 509 system, it was immediately noted that there was a POI signal source named "Perpetual Toil" orbiting body B 2 a, along with a Convoy Beacon orbiting body A 2.

When dropping into the "Perpetual Toil" signal source, you will immediately see a very rare Collinson-Class Asteroid Ship.

Upon inspection of the exterior of the megaship, it was discovered that there were 6 Ship Log Uplinks present, 5 of which ould be scanned for logs concerning the megaships activites. (Note: The Uplink on the Bow of the ship does not contain a log.)

Perpetual Toil - Log Locations
PERPETUAL TOIL 1/5

Perpetual Toil log #237. Captain Olivia Mara, September 4th 3254.

Another day, another credit. l'm calculating our tonnage and things are looking rosy. The mining crews have outdone themselves this quarter - we're making excellent progress in our asteroid. I've never seen so many gemstones in a single rock. Ten percent on this haul is gonna make everyone real happy.

The new hydroponics dome is finished, so the crew will be able to supplement rations with fresh fruit and vegetables in a few weeks. The logistics chief, Andros, is organising a celebration in the mess hall tonight. I don't mind saying on record that I'm tired of reheated protein packs. Maybe for our next trip the company will give us sealed meals that look vaguely like the picture on the label? A girl can dream.

One matter to note: Mining Team B stumbled upon a cavern deep in the rock. There's evidence of liquid dripping down the walls. I've asked for structural surveys before anyone ventures further in there. Our initial scans didn't catch any interior spaces, so I want to be certain that further mining won't destabilise the surrounding area.

Anyway, that's all for now. I'm heading down to the mess hall. I hope Andros allocated this party some wine.

Image: PERPETUAL TOIL 1/5

PERPETUAL TOIL 2/5

Perpetual Toil log #238. Captain Olivia Mara, September 6th.

Mining Crew B, uh... They found something in the cavern.

While the structural surveys were taking place, the shift leader reported a substance seeping from the rock face. 'Like blood', she said. At first they thought it was a sealed water reservoir or something, but it wasn't. I saw the camera feed. It was too thick.

l've now read the eyewitness reports three times and I still don't know what to make of them. One miner describes an ooze, like unrefined oil. Another swears it was a swarm of insects, crawling and writhing over each other. A third simply logged a single word: 'Demons'.

Maybe the mind plays tricks down in the dark. But everyone who saw it at least agreed on one thing. It - whatever 'it' is - appears to be alive. We've collected a sample of the life form. It's down in the lab now. Science Chief Pendleton is making sure it's not dangerous. We ran health checks on everyone in Crew B and found nothing concerning. Looks like we got lucky.

l've told no one at the company yet. My gut tells me they'll shut down this operation and kick everyone off the ship if we report a biological discovery. Meaning we won't get our cut of the gems we've already pulled up. Well, screw that. I'm not letting my crew get shafted by red tape.

I wonder what the bonus would be if we analysed the life form ourselves...?

Image: PERPETUAL TOIL 2/5

PERPETUAL TOIL 3/5

Perpetual Toil log #239. Captain Olivia Mara. September 9th.

Okay. I need to get this down while it's still fresh, even though part of me would rather never have to think about it again.

Two days ago, I made the call to send Mining Crew B back into that cavern. Pendleton didn't find anything dangerous in the sample they collected, and we've got a mission schedule to follow. I keep telling myself it was the right thing to do.

This morning Crew B's shift leader sent a garbled message. Said the life form in the cavern was "moving wrong." Then... nothing. Comms went dead mid-sentence. I sent a security detail after them. Standard procedure: six people, fully equipped for search and rescue.

Three came back.

I've spoken to each and almost wish I hadn't. All three agree Crew B were already dead when they arrived, and that's the only consistent detail they'll give. Nobody agrees on HOW the miners died! One insists the walls were "breathing." Another keeps watching the corner of the med bay. They can't stop shaking. I... I don't know what to make of it all.

I've sealed the main access door between us and the asteroid interior. Pulled all other mining crews out. Whatever's in there stays in there, away from my ship.

The company expects an update by the end of the week. My crew will want reassurances that I can't give to them about our safety. And I keep coming back to the same thought, over and over: I sent Crew B back in.

I did that.

Image: PERPETUAL TOIL 3/5

PERPETUAL TOIL 4/5

Perpetual Toll log #240. Captain Olivia Mara. September...

Date is... I can't recall.

The life form sample we took. Stored in the lab, behind reinforced glass. That Pendleton swore was contained. It started to hum. A low buzzing at the edge of your hearing, like an itch under your skull that wouldn't stop. We ran it through every audio filter we had to try and shut it out, but nothing worked. Just a constant hum that crawled under the skin.

People who heard it said it felt like being watched. l just told them to focus and get on with their jobs. Then the life form in the asteroid came passed through the sealed door. Through solid bulkhead. It's on my ship! And it's slaughtering my crew! The.... The reports coming up from the lower decks aren't reports anymore. They're just screams.

We sent out a distress call. For whatever that's worth I've given the order to get to the shuttles and abandon the Toil. Fewer than a hundred are going to reach them from secured areas.

The rest... the decks where that thing is, I've sealed them. Over half my crew is trapped there.

Save who I can, right? Isn't that what a captain's supposed to do? Assess the situation. Make hard calls. Think of people as numbers. Follow protocol.

I could hear those still alive crying for help through comms as the shuttles fired up. Begging me to let them into the landing bays. But they already knew the truth.

Further Investigation

Upon further investigation into the LFT 509 system, it was noted that there was a Human signal present on the body B 3 b. After surface mapping the body, a new POI was noted to be present, the "Silver-Six Observatory".

After landing and scouting with an SRV, it was determined that there were 10 Log Uplinks present, however only 5 of which could be scanned for logs concerning what had happened at the Observatory.

Silver-Six Observatory - Log Locations
SILVER-SIX OBSERVATORY 1/5

Personal log, Professor Wilfred Norris. November 23rd, 3254.

Forgive the background racket. They're still bolting the containment wing together around me.

A confession for the record, one I would never offer to an Imperial oversight committee: I haven't slept properly in four days.

Thirty-one years spent cataloguing extremophiles and building theories about exogenous biology, authoring papers that a few dozen people might idly read while the weather is poor. Then this! A truly new life form, sitting in a system that is now permit locked from the rest of the galaxy.

It's all mine to study... Ours, I mean.

The survivors of the Perpetual Toil wait in orbit aboard their ships while we build. Standard quarantine procedures are in place - the Toil's crew are not permitted to leave their vessel until we have facilities to safely hold and screen them.

Details provided by Captain Olivia Mara lead me to believe that boarding the Toll would be significantly hazardous. l have assured my superiors that the life form can be studied safely, given time to assess and plan.

Silver-Six Observatory should be ready within the week. I hope the descriptions given to us by the Toil's logs are not another terror-fuelled exaggeration. The alien life form's stated qualities for both survivability and violence are fascinating.

Image: SILVER-SIX OBSERVATORY 1/5

SILVER-SIX OBSERVATORY 2/5

Personal log, Professor Wilfred Norris. November 29th, 3254.

This afternoon, as the final screws and seals were applied within Silver-Six Observatory, an unexpected development occurred. A panicked series of messages from one of the shuttles holding the Toil's crew reached us as settlement comms came online.

A junior technician of the Toil's science team had a biological sample! A sealed flask contained a portion of the life form, smuggled aboard his shuttle and hidden for weeks. Weeks! Sitting among the survivors while none of them had the faintest idea.

When the flask was discovered... Well, they beat the man rather badly, then ejected the flask into space before my people could get there. I had it scooped up and brought directly into a secure lab.

Are we looking at one life form or multiple symbiotes separated into different states? Does it, or they, have a sense of self? My study of the Toil's recordings and test data has led me to believe the alien possesses some degree of intelligence and autonomy.

The survivors are finally down from orbit and quarantined in the west wing. The life form specimen remains isolated in the east wing. That junior technician remains in my thoughts, however.

When I asked him why he'd hide the bio sample, in spite of every scientific regulation and scrap of self-preservation known to him... The young man replied that he "heard his mother" inside a hum emitted by the life form. A woman who died eleven years ago, it seems.

I classified this as post-traumatic stress after the events of the Toll. That's a reasonable interpretation, isn't it? Tomorrow we begin multi-spectrum observation and I want a clear head. I've waiting my whole career for what happens next.

Image: SILVER-SIX OBSERVATORY 2/5

SILVER-SIX OBSERVATORY 3/5

Personal log, Professor Wilfred Norris. December 5th, 3254.

Six days of exobiological lstudy and half my assumptions are already in the bin. Which is exactly the point of science and discovery!

The specimen appears docile, as far as I can determine. It sits in containment and "shifts"; that's the only word that seems fitting. Solid to liquid to something close to gas, then back again. A xenobiological stimulus-response assessment yields no information about what triggers these 'shifts'. The specimen simply does what it does, when it does.

Occasionally, it extends part of itself toward the containment bay. My notes describe it as probing, though I dislike that term since it implies motive. It reaches, rests against the containment barrier for a moment, then draws back. The alien reminds me of a captive octopus testing the edges of their tanks. Watching it can calm the nerves, if I'm honest.

The survivors, however, are another matter. The west wing has filed formal complaints about their quarters. I'll admit that construction was rushed but material comforts came second to the research facilities. Science first. Always.

Captain Mara requested a call this afternoon to discuss care arrangements for her remaining crew. l'll speak to her at the observation station during our next spectral analysis pass.

For now, back to the observatory. The life form reaching for the surface again. I could watch it for hours.

Image: SILVER-SIX OBSERVATORY 3/5

SILVER-SIX OBSERVATORY 4/5

Personal log, Professor Wilfred Norris. December 5th, 3254.

I need to set this down exactly, while the order of events is still fresh in my mind. There has been a disturbing yet unforeseen development.

Captain Mara's call came through while I was at the observation station. The specimen rested in front of me. No state 'shifts'. Just more routinely lethargic probing of its containment bay.

The instant Mara's voice came through the speaker - the very same moment - everything changed.

The probing stopped. The life form gathered itself, growing still. Then it moved, like tumbling sand, straight toward a seam in the containment bay that l have been assured was secure. It must have found a weakness during all those hours of gentle probing I found so soothing to watch. Within seconds it was gone!

I stood there, trying to assess what had spurred the life form I'd been calmly cataloguing to slipped containment so easily. Then Captain Mara spoke again, asking why I had fallen silent.

Just minutes later, the life form appeared in the west wing, via the air ducts. And it... Excuse me.

It killed them. All of them. Every survivor from the Perpetual Toil, Captain Mara included.

When the slaughter was over, the life form showed no interest in anybody else. Not the scientists who watched in horror. Not the security team with weapons pointed at it through glass. It didn't even try to leave the quarantine wing. It unleashed a savagely efficient wrath over the humans who had invaded its asteroid, finishing what it started on the Toll.

And then it stopped.

Even now it rests, gently shifting forms in a lazy, irregular pattern. I ordered the air duct welded shut. I don't know why I bothered. If it can find a seam our head engineer certified, a new weld won't mean much.

The alien remains here because, apparently, it has chosen to. I no longer find its ponderous movement soothing.

Image: SILVER-SIX OBSERVATORY 4/5

SILVER-SIX OBSERVATORY 5/5

Personal log, Professor Wilfred Norris. December 11th, 3254.

I'll keep this simple. The life form has become active.

After days of stillness, it started testing the seals again. That same slow probing as previously described. I understand now what that behaviour is. It isn't aimless, innate curiosity.

A predator is learning its surroundings.

Behaviour, of course, demonstrated before its escape and subsequent massacre of the Perpetual ToiI survivors. I won't wait to see what it decides to do next. Silver-Six Observatory is to be abandoned, effective immediately. My staff are already boarding the shuttles. I will be the last to leave.

Whoever finds this, and I assume someone will eventually: This is not a specimen to be studied. Both here and on the Toil, the alien went where it wished, when it wished. Several attempts at containment, from megaship bulkheads to state-of-the-art exobiological quarantine bays, failed. The alien chooses its own path, ignorant of our intentions.

As triumphant as humanity's scientific revolution is and continues to be, I do not trust us to engage with this entity on any productive level. I will not exterminate it - genocide would be a base, flawed response to an unknown life form's obvious distress at the ruin of its asteroid home.

My recommendation is simple. The system must remain permit locked. For good. I don't know how long it can survive. I don't even know if it can die at all. I only know it must never be allowed to engage with humanity on a wider scale. Such an event could be catastrophic.

Convoy Beacon

The previously mentioned Convoy Beacon that is present with the LFT 509 system can be found around body A 2.

Upon entering the Convoy Beacon signal source, you will find a Type-9 being escorted by two Anaconda's. The Type-9 will usually have a few AI Relics (between 4-8) within its cargo hold, along with some Aluminium (between 16-24).

However, while this Convoy Beacon is present with the same system, it is not believed to be related to the events of the Perpetual Toil or Silver-Six Observatory, as it has only been present within the system for several years, since 10th March 3301.
